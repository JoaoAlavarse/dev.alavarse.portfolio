# Engineering Portfolio Specification (EPS)

Version: 1.0
Status: Living Document

# 1. Purpose

## Objective

This portfolio exists to demonstrate how João Alavarse makes Software Engineering decisions.

Its purpose is not to present a collection of technologies, projects or professional experiences.

Instead, it aims to make visible the reasoning behind technical decisions, the way problems are understood and the engineering principles that guide software development in production environments.

Every page should contribute to answering one question:

> Why should someone trust João's engineering judgment?

The portfolio should leave the visitor with confidence not only in João's technical ability, but also in his ability to understand problems, evaluate alternatives, communicate decisions and build software that remains maintainable over time.

---

# 2. Problem Statement

Most software engineering portfolios present:

- technologies
- repositories
- certificates
- screenshots
- project lists

These artifacts demonstrate activity.

They rarely demonstrate engineering judgment.

Engineering, however, is fundamentally a discipline of decision making.

The portfolio therefore exists to solve a different problem:

> Make engineering judgment observable.

---

# 3. Positioning

João is positioned as a Software Engineer who explains engineering through real systems.

Technology is never presented as an end.

Projects are not presented as achievements.

Every project is evidence of how engineering decisions are made.

The portfolio should communicate that João works by understanding:

- people
- business
- context
- constraints
- consequences

before choosing an implementation.

---

# 4. Engineering Thesis

## Core Thesis

Good engineering begins before implementation.

Code is only one consequence of engineering.

Engineering begins when someone understands:

- who is affected
- what problem exists
- why it exists
- what constraints exist
- what trade-offs are acceptable
- what consequences every decision creates

Implementation comes afterwards.

This thesis must be demonstrated throughout the portfolio.

It should almost never need to be explicitly stated.

---

# 5. Design Principles

Every content decision should follow these principles.

## Principle 1

Evidence over claims.

Never state a competence that cannot be observed.

---

## Principle 2

Context before implementation.

Always explain why something existed before explaining how it was built.

---

## Principle 3

Decisions before technologies.

Technologies explain implementation.

Decisions explain engineering.

---

## Principle 4

Reality over perfection.

Real projects include uncertainty, constraints and compromises.

Those elements increase credibility.

---

## Principle 5

Complexity requires justification.

If something became complex, explain why.

---

## Principle 6

Engineering is collaborative.

Whenever true, show interaction with:

- product
- support
- business
- users
- operations

Software never exists in isolation.

---

## Principle 7

Teaching is engineering.

Communication is treated as an engineering competency.

Teaching demonstrates understanding.

Documentation demonstrates leadership.

# 11. Trust Model

Trust is built through consistency.

Every statement belongs to one of four categories.

## Verified Fact

Supported by documentation or public evidence.

Examples:

- employment
- dates
- public talks
- published projects

---

## Observed Result

Can be directly observed.

Examples:

- migration completed
- documentation produced
- application deployed

---

## Engineering Interpretation

Reasonable conclusion derived from evidence.

Example:

Incremental migration demonstrates concern for delivery risk.

---

## Opinion

Personal belief.

Opinions should rarely appear.

When they do, they should be clearly separated from facts.

---

# 12. Content Rules

The portfolio should never:

- exaggerate seniority
- invent metrics
- inflate responsibilities
- use marketing language
- list technologies as achievements
- present responsibilities without context

The portfolio should always:

- explain decisions
- acknowledge uncertainty
- distinguish facts from interpretation
- show trade-offs
- prioritize clarity

---

# 13. Case Publication Criteria

A case may be published only if it contains:

- clear context
- identifiable problem
- João's real role
- at least one engineering decision
- observable result or current state
- no confidential information

Otherwise it remains an internal draft.

---

# 14. Quality Criteria

The portfolio succeeds if:

A recruiter understands João in less than five minutes.

A Technical Lead finishes a case believing the decisions were reasonable.

A developer learns something independently of the technology used.

No section depends on self-promotion to communicate competence.

Competence emerges naturally from evidence.

---

# 15. Evolution Strategy

The portfolio grows by increasing evidence.

Never by increasing claims.

Priority order:

1. Better engineering cases.
2. Better documentation.
3. Better explanations.
4. Better visual presentation.
5. New technologies.

Technology is always the last priority.

---

# 16. Decision Records

Major editorial decisions should be documented.

Each record should contain:

- Decision
- Context
- Alternatives
- Choice
- Consequences

- Reversibility

This section evolves over time.

# Appendix A — Editorial Checklist

Before publishing any content, verify:

- Does this strengthen the engineering thesis?
- Is there observable evidence?
- Is context explained before implementation?
- Are technologies supporting the narrative instead of leading it?
- Are responsibilities accurately represented?
- Does this teach something?
- Would a Technical Lead find this credible?
- Would a recruiter understand it quickly?
- Does this avoid unnecessary self-promotion?

If any answer is "no", the content should be revised before publication.
